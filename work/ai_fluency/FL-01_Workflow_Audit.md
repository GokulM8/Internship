## 2. Task Classification Principles

### Just me

Tasks where the final decision, judgment, responsibility, or learning outcome should remain with me.

### Delegate to AI with review

Tasks where AI can perform most of the initial work, but I must inspect and approve the result.

### Collaborate with AI

Tasks where AI and I work iteratively, combining AI assistance with human reasoning and judgment.

### Fully automate

Tasks that are repetitive, predictable, and low-risk enough to run automatically with minimal human intervention.

For my current workflow, I have intentionally not classified any major task as fully automated because most of my work involves learning, technical judgment, or research interpretation.

## 3. Three Target Tasks for FL-02 → FL-04

### Target Task 1 — ML/Code Debugging

**AI role:** Collaborate with AI.

**Done well means:**
- The root cause of the error is identified.
- The proposed fix works in my actual environment.
- Existing functionality is not unnecessarily broken.
- I understand why the fix works.
- Relevant tests or execution complete successfully.

**Success definition:**

A debugging session is successful when the original error is resolved, the relevant code execution or test passes, and I can explain the cause and fix in my own words.

### Target Task 2 — ML Data Analysis & Interpretation

**AI role:** Collaborate with AI.

**Done well means:**
- Important data-quality issues are identified.
- Class distributions and relevant statistics are correctly calculated.
- AI-generated interpretations are checked against the actual data.
- No unsupported conclusions are presented.
- Visualizations communicate important findings clearly.

**Success definition:**

An analysis is successful when key dataset characteristics are documented, reported statistics can be reproduced from the data, and major interpretations are supported by actual analysis or visualization.

### Target Task 3 — Technical/Research Writing

**AI role:** Delegate to AI with review.

**Done well means:**
- The document accurately describes the actual project.
- No experimental results or claims are fabricated.
- The methodology is reproducible.
- Results use actual measurements.
- Limitations are stated honestly.
- A technical reader can understand the methodology and workflow.

**Success definition:**

A document is successful when every important technical claim can be traced to my project, data, code, or experiment, and another technically capable person can understand the methodology and reproduce the main workflow.

## 4. Where I Will Not Delegate to AI

I will retain final responsibility for:

- Research conclusions
- Important technical decisions
- Verification of experimental results
- Deciding whether evidence supports a claim
- Final submission of academic and internship work
- Handling private or sensitive information
- Decisions where an incorrect AI answer could materially affect the outcome

AI can assist my reasoning, but it should not replace my responsibility for the final result.
