Content Refresh Prioritization: Predicting Forward Search Decline from March-Only Signals

A public-safe, decision-support study asking whether signals available at the end of March can help identify webpages whose organic clicks decline in April.

March → April 2026 Binary classification Client-grouped validation Human review required

Abstract

This study investigates whether March-only search and engagement signals can identify webpages likely to experience a forward decline in organic clicks, supporting human-reviewed content refresh prioritization. A Random Forest classifier using five March 2026 features was evaluated against a fixed Week-4 rule-based baseline on a client-grouped held-out split. On this March-to-April slice, the model achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.953919 and Precision@10 of 1.000, compared with 0.686792 and 0.700 for the baseline. Validation audits showed a higher ROC-AUC of approximately 0.964 under a naive random split and approximately 0.968 when March total clicks was deliberately added as a leaky feature, reinforcing the need for grouped validation and careful feature design. The resulting ranking is a directional decision-support signal for human review, not evidence of causality, guaranteed traffic recovery, or an automated content-editing system.

01 · Question

Which pages deserve attention first?

The research question is: which webpages should be prioritized for content refresh to maximize future organic search performance?

ML task Binary classification of forward click decline.
Decision A weekly/monthly queue for a content strategist.
Scope Decision support, not auto-publishing or auto-editing.

The target is is_declining_forward = 1 when a page's total clicks in April 2026 are lower than its total clicks in March 2026. The model uses only information observable by the end of March.

Windows & exclusions

  • March 2026 → model features
  • April 2026 → forward outcome only
  • June 2026 sample/sealed month → explicitly excluded
  • Rows missing either March or April → excluded rather than imputed

Leakage boundary. April information is never used as a model feature. March total clicks is also excluded from the final model because the label directly compares March and April click totals.

Primary validation uses GroupShuffleSplit with client_hash_id as the grouping variable and a 30% held-out test set. The audited split contained no client overlap between train and test.

Baseline

The fixed Week-4 rule is re-scored on the same held-out split. It is not retrained for the final comparison.

Validation and leakage audit

The naive random split produced approximately 0.964 ROC-AUC, compared with approximately 0.953 under the client-grouped split used for the main result. This difference is consistent with the stricter grouping requirement: pages from the same client cannot contribute to both sides of the evaluation.

A separate deliberate leakage experiment added march_total_clicks and produced approximately 0.968 ROC-AUC. This is not reported as model performance; it is an audit showing why the feature is excluded from the final model.

What the model appears to use

Permutation importance shows avg_ctr as the dominant measured feature in this run, with a mean ROC-AUC drop of approximately 0.193 when permuted. The remaining features are much smaller in this single evaluation and should not be interpreted as universal drivers.

Error analysis

The notebook's strongest evidence is the aggregate held-out evaluation and its validation/leakage audits. This artifact therefore avoids inventing page-level error narratives that are not supported by the executed outputs.

05 · Limitations

Strong result, deliberately narrow claim

One month pair

The study covers March → April 2026. It does not establish behavior across seasons, other months, or future search changes.

Not causal

The model identifies correlated pre-decline signals. It does not prove that refreshing a page causes traffic to recover.

Precision@10 context

Precision@10 must be interpreted together with the held-out test-set size and the exact ranking procedure; it is not a universal guarantee.

Not production infrastructure

The output is a research artifact and prioritization aid. Human review remains mandatory before any content action.

Honest framing: the measured performance is specific to this March-to-April slice and this client-grouped evaluation. It should be treated as directional evidence for prioritization rather than a guarantee of future traffic behavior.

06 · Action Playbook

Turn the score into a review queue

The final model produces a decline probability that is converted into interpretable archetypes. The ranking is designed to help a strategist decide what to inspect first—not what the system should automatically change.

01

Declining / High-Volume

Prioritize human review when decline probability is high and the page has meaningful search exposure. Investigate content freshness, intent alignment, SERP competition, and recent changes before deciding on a refresh.

REFRESH_CONTENT
02

Good Position / CTR Gap

For pages already ranking in positions 1–10 but showing a below-median CTR for their position bucket, inspect titles and meta descriptions as a potential optimization opportunity.

OPTIMIZE_TITLE_META
03

Stable / Performing

Keep the page in normal monitoring rather than spending refresh capacity immediately.

NO_ACTION
04

Low-Volume / Low-Confidence

Treat low-impression pages cautiously. A high score on little search exposure is weaker evidence for immediate intervention.

MONITOR_ONLY

Never automated: publishing or rewriting content without human editing; deleting or deindexing pages from the score alone; acting on a single month without additional confirmation; treating low-volume pages as equally trustworthy; or using the queue as the sole input for client-facing spend or contract decisions.

Evaluation recipe

  1. Aggregate March 2026 page-level signals.
  2. Join April 2026 totals only to construct the forward label.
  3. Split by client_hash_id with a 30% held-out group.
  4. Train the specified Random Forest on the five March-only features.
  5. Compare against the fixed baseline on the identical held-out rows.
  6. Run the split and leakage audits and preserve the generated artifacts.
